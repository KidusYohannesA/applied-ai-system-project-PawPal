## Puppy / kitten stage (0-12 months)

Young animals need more frequent feeding, more frequent vaccination boosters, and more attentive socialization. Schedule densities are highest at this stage. Most care tasks scale to "high" priority and shorter durations.

## Young adult stage (1-3 years)

Highest exercise needs of any life stage. Establishing routines now (twice-daily feeding, daily walks, weekly grooming touch) prevents most adult behavioral and health issues. Most care tasks are daily-frequency, high-priority.

## Mature adult stage (3-7 years for dogs, 3-10 for cats)

The "stable" middle stage — routines from young adulthood continue, with portion sizes often adjusting downward as activity drops. Annual vet visits remain important.

## Senior stage (7+ for large dogs, 10+ for small dogs and cats)

Senior animals usually need more frequent vet visits (every 6 months), stricter weight management, joint supplements, and gentler exercise (shorter, more frequent walks rather than fewer long ones). Many medications start at this stage. Mobility-related grooming (mat prevention in cats that stop self-grooming, nail trims that prevent slipping) becomes more important.
