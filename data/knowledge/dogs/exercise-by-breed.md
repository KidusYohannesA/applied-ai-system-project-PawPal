## High-energy working and herding breeds

Working and herding breeds (Border Collie, Australian Shepherd, German Shepherd, Husky, Belgian Malinois) typically need 60-120 minutes of vigorous daily exercise split across at least two sessions. Adult dogs of these breeds rarely thrive on a single 20-30 minute walk; under-exercise is the most common cause of destructive behavior at home. A "walk" task for these breeds should usually be 45-60 minutes and scheduled as daily, frequency=daily, priority=high.

## Medium-energy active breeds

Active medium-energy breeds (Labrador Retriever, Golden Retriever, Shiba Inu, Beagle, Boxer) need 45-60 minutes of daily exercise. Two 30-minute walks (morning + evening) is a reliable default. Younger adults (1-4 years) often need closer to 60 minutes; seniors (8+) closer to 30. Recommended frequency=daily, priority=high.

## Low-energy and brachycephalic breeds

Low-energy and short-nosed breeds (Bulldog, French Bulldog, Pug, Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, Basset Hound) do well with two 15-20 minute walks per day. Brachycephalic dogs especially must avoid heat and exertion in temperatures above 75F. Recommended duration=15-20 min, frequency=daily, priority=high.

## Toy and small breeds

Toy breeds (Chihuahua, Yorkshire Terrier, Maltese, Pomeranian) need 20-30 minutes of daily exercise, often split into two short walks plus indoor play. They are sensitive to cold and to long walks on hot pavement. Recommended duration=15 min per session, frequency=daily, priority=medium-high.

## Puppies — exercise scaling rule

For puppies under 18 months, a common veterinary guideline is roughly 5 minutes of structured exercise per month of age, twice a day (e.g., a 4-month-old puppy: ~20 min twice daily). Excessive forced exercise on developing joints can damage growth plates. Recommended priority=high, frequency=daily, duration scaled to age.
